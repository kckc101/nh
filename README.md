# E-Rave Cambodia — Web Client 🎧🇰🇭

The browser side of **E-Rave Cambodia**, a virtual neon rave festival held at night in **Morodok Techo National Stadium**, Phnom Penh. Visitors build a blocky Roblox-style raver, drop onto the pitch in front of an Angkor-crowned mega-stage, and dance to music the host DJ controls.

The multiplayer server lives in **[kckc101/hh](https://github.com/kckc101/hh)**. Without it, the client runs as an offline demo: simulated crowd, built-in music, and local DJ controls.

## Run locally

```bash
# 1. start the server (in a clone of kckc101/hh)
npm install && npm run dev        # → http://localhost:3001

# 2. start this web client
npm install && npm run dev        # → http://localhost:5173
```

- Open **http://localhost:5173**. Vite proxies `/socket.io`, `/api` and `/uploads` to the server on port 3001. Set `SERVER_URL` to proxy somewhere else.
- Host mode: **http://localhost:5173/?dj=true** and enter the server's DJ password.
- Phones on the same Wi-Fi: use the `Network:` URL Vite prints.

## Deploy

```bash
npm run build                     # → dist/
```

Pick one:

- **GitHub Pages:** `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` builds and publishes on every push to `main`. Set Pages' source to "GitHub Actions". The site runs the offline demo unless the repository variable `VITE_SERVER_URL` points at a hosted server.
- **Same origin:** let the server host the build: `STATIC_DIR=../nh/dist npm start` in the hh repo.
- **Separate hosts** (e.g. GitHub Pages / Netlify + Render): build with `VITE_SERVER_URL=https://your-server.example npm run build`. The build uses relative asset paths, so it also works from a sub-path.

Nothing of the rave runs behind it: no 3D scene, no music, no multiplayer connection. | Venue | **Morodok Techo National Stadium** (11.68289°N 104.87628°E), on a cyberpunk night: navy-black sky, cyan `#00f3ff` / magenta `#ff007f` rim light. The geometry lives in one file, `src/world/venue.js`, with a 68 × 105 m pitch, an 8-lane track and a three-tier elliptical ("capsule") bowl (`environment.js`). The stands are packed (`src/world/fans.js`). The lower tier holds up to 5,000 blocky 3D ravers in the avatars' proportions and random outfits, at 96 vertices each (five boxes, hidden faces left out). The middle and upper tiers are about 10,600 flat sprites, one vertex each, with the same outfits and moves. They face the stage and dance on the beat: bouncing, fist-pumping, hands up (more of them in a drop, and half of them jump), some filming on phone torches or waving glowsticks. All the motion runs in the vertex shaders. The broadcast cameras see sprite stand-ins instead of the 3D ravers (except the pit cam, in among them). Graphics quality thins them (65% on Medium, 40% on Low), and so does the performance governor's `fanShare`: down to 25% in steps while FPS < 45, and back up when it's smooth. The pitch is kept clear for the stage and players walking: every simulated raver is in the stands (`Crowd.spawnBot`, `StandCrowd.allocateSeat`). Three in four are in the lower tier's front rows and the rest in the middle tier, all the way round the bowl, dancing on the spot facing the DJ. They sit down now and then (more in breakdowns) and all stand for a drop. Every 8th parties on a VIP deck instead. They have name tags and private chat like anyone else. The bowl also has a scrolling LED ribbon board, translucent cable-stayed roof canopies (ribbed membranes the stands show through from above) over the east and west stands, the two 99 m curved **Sampeah pylons** (palms pressed together) behind the north and south goals with neon edges, stay cables and aviation lights, and a Phnom Penh skyline beyond. The stage sits on the centre spot facing north, and the **dance floor** is the north half of the pitch, a neon grid with beat tiles and bass rings. You arrive seated in a free seat in the lower tier's front rows at the north end, facing the DJ, with the stage front and the LED wall in view (`World.spawnSeat`, clear of other players). The intro shot glides down to your seat. From there you can walk the pitch and track; the stands' front wall and the crowd barrier hold them in. **VIP decks** on the first tier, east and west of the halfway line, have glass rails, sofas, a neon "VIP" sign and a ramp up from the track. The stands are walkable too, tier by tier: you get there by sitting down, from the back of a VIP deck, or off the side of its ramp. You can't climb a wall between levels, but you can hop down off the front of a tier toward the pitch. Wherever you are in the middle or upper tier, the sprites around you make way for about 400 real 3D fans. Every 8th simulated raver parties up there. On arrival, the camera swoops in from a high establishing shot (about 60° pitch, pylons in frame) down to your raver. |
| Strobe lighting (DJ) | The DJ panel's **LIGHTING** section: **Normal** (festival lighting), **Auto strobe** (pitch black with a white flash on every bar, beat or 1/8) and **Blackout** (dark until the DJ holds **HOLD TO FLASH**, or the H key; L cycles modes). Flash length 50–100 ms. Flashes are quantized to the beat clock (60000 / BPM, phase-locked to the kick). Blackout skips drawing the frame and clears to #000, so it's truly black and free. Flashes write only to existing lights and uniforms — no allocations. The state is server-synced, so everyone's room strobes together. "Reduce flashing lights" caps it at 2 dimmer flashes per second. Code: `src/world/strobe.js` (LightingController), wired in `World.frame()`; rules in `shared/lighting.js`. **Arena Blackout** (the switch at the top of LIGHTING, or **K**; B stays the drone) is separate from these modes and works with any of them. It kills the house lights: the hemisphere and rim lights go to 0, the sky dome (stars, moon, horizon glow) and the city skyline fade to `#020205`, and so do the background and fog. The crowd's own glow goes too: the stand fans' sky light, palette rims, LED tops, phone torches and glowsticks, the avatars' neon, VIP auras and profile photos. Everything the show drives stays at full power: moving heads, lasers and the emerald array, beams, blinders, the front strobe wash, the LED wall and screens, the dance floor, cryo and pyro, and the VIP spotlight. So the crowd is lit only by the stage. The switch is synced (`arena` in the server's light state, `shared/lighting.js`), so newcomers join into it, and ravers get a short heads-up. It eases in over 0.35 s and back out over 0.5 s (smoothstep, so it punches without popping). Everything is scaled by one eased level (`mood.arena`, the shared `crowdGlow` uniform) rather than overwritten, so switching it off restores exactly what was there; there's no cache to go stale. |
| Lights & FX | Lit like an exclusive club night: mostly darkness, one curated look at a time (red room, UV, ice, magenta, amber, acid — each a hue plus white), and the full rig saved for the drop. Narrow moving heads on the front truss (plus pitch-side towers that open up on drops) with floor light pools, **spotlight arrays on top of both pylons** (4 each, 90 m up) sweeping inward in a figure-of-eight over the dance floor, chasing on the beat and colouring from the current look, subtle LED halo rings over the crowd, a faint laser "liquid sky" sheet in breakdowns, a single-colour laser rig (front-of-house towers fire back at the stage on drops), the emerald mainstage laser array and the drop / breakdown cues (below), audience blinders (white on a drop), warm amber festoon bulbs, LED floor tiles on the beat, strobes, fire cannons, CO₂ cryo jets along the barrier, smoke, confetti, fireworks, bloom. All are driven by live kick detection and the track's arrangement. |
| Drop & breakdown cues | The big festival moments, on top of the lights (`src/world/lightingdirector.js`, `LightingDirector`). **`triggerDrop()`**: the room's ambient light drops to 0.05 (the neon rims dim with it), the audience blinders punch **white** on the next four beats, the **cryo jets** fire, and the lasers go to full power. The mainstage array alternates SkyFan and CrowdSlice every 4 bars, with a StrobeBlink fill on the last beat of every 8, and the laser show goes to full. **`triggerBreakdown()`**: the lasers sink to a faint, slow, low sweep, the room and the whole rig (beams, LED wall, floor, stands) go to **deep purples and blues**, and the haze thickens and drifts slowly. **`blackout(beats)`**: a stage blackout. On the built-in synth tracks the last beat of every build goes black, so the drop lands out of the dark. The music drives it all: the arrangement's sections pick the look, and a drop section (or an energy surge on YouTube and other tracks) fires the hit, with auto-FX on. The DJ calls **DROP** (held 16 bars) and **BREAKDOWN** (8 bars) for everyone from the top of the DJ panel's STAGE FX pad. Looks blend in fast for a drop and slowly for a breakdown. A VIP show overrides them, and "Reduce flashing lights" caps the hits. **Mainstage laser array** (`src/world/laserrig.js`, `LaserRig`): 24 heads (16–32) along the front lip of the stage, neon emerald `#00FF44` by default, configurable colour, beam width, pitch, spread and sweep speed. It has three looks: **SkyFan** (up into the sky in a broad V that opens and closes with the beat), **CrowdSlice** (flat over the dance floor, a scissor sweep rising to the stands' eye level) and **StrobeBlink** (a wide fan blinking on 16th notes; half notes and dimmer with "Reduce flashing lights"). Looks blend over half a second. **Cryo jets** (`src/world/cryojets.js`, `CryoJets`): 10 CO₂ jets (8–12) in the photo pit behind the crowd barrier. A burst is a narrow white column shot up at 26–34 m/s that swells to about 3 m across at the top, about 11 m up; every particle lives under 1.2 s. Patterns are all at once, a chase across the stage, middle out, or odd then even. The CO2 Jets pad and every drop fire them. **Beams on every screen:** every laser (the array and the 96-beam show) is a `BeamBatch`, one instanced draw of camera-facing cones. Each has a hot core in a soft glow skirt, fades into the air and is fogged. It's never drawn thinner than about 1.25 px of the target it's rendering into, with its brightness spread to match, so beams stay clean on the main view and on the broadcast cameras' 640 × 360 feeds instead of breaking up. The broadcast renders also size every point sprite (the CO₂ plumes, sparks, the stand sprites) for their own target and lens; before this they came out about 3× too big on the LED screens. The screens' shader has a soft knee over 1, so a laser's hot core keeps its colour on the wall instead of clipping to white. |
| Laser show // Main drop | A five-phase drop the DJ runs from the panel's **LASER SHOW // MAIN DROP** section, for everyone (`src/world/lasershow.js`, `LaserChoreographySystem`; rules in `shared/show.js`). **1 TEASE** (16 beats): a deep purple and warm gold wash, white moving heads stabbing in fast criss-crosses on the half beat, and one beat of full-stage green laser on beat 8. **2 BLINDER** (4 beats): lasers cut, white blinders, a fast white strobe and the bloom swelling into the drop. **3 LASER DROP** (32 beats): a dark violet wash and every laser tier in neon green `#00FF33`, by turns slow upward-sweeping liquid-sky sheets and wide oscillating aerial beam matrices (8 beats each); the LED wall goes to its hardcore program. **4 PYRO GATE** (32 beats): the green lasers gated on the kick (on with it, off on the off-beat) and re-aimed every beat, flame bursts every 4th beat, CO₂ plumes every 8th and white strobe hits. **5 OUTRO** (32 beats): green out, slow narrow white beams sweeping the sky, gold sparkle bursts high over the stage, and a magenta and violet wash. **Controls:** **AUTO DROP SEQUENCE** runs 1 → 5 and hands back to the normal show, with the phase, beat and a progress bar shown live. The five pads start their phase on the next beat and loop it (during AUTO they jump the sequence there). **BLACKOUT / RESET** gives two beats of black, then the normal show. The server stamps every command with the next beat of the track playing (its BPM, counted from its start), so every client's lasers, flames and plumes land on the same beat. **The rig:** 18 emitters in three tiers, all one instanced draw: 6 centre-low behind the booth (tight X cross-beams, 15–35° up), 4 + 4 on the wings up the front truss towers (sheets and beam arrays over the crowd), and 4 crown units on top of the front truss (steep aerial beams). Each is a fan of discrete rays or a solid liquid-sky sheet. The shader (additive, no depth write, double-sided) fades along the beam into the sky (`pow(1 − v, uFadePower)`), softens to nothing at the sheet's sides, fades a sheet seen edge-on (the drone flying through it), and drifts with haze. Rays are anti-aliased and keep their energy when sub-pixel. The rest of the show (moving heads, blinders, strobe, the emerald array, flames, CO₂, the palette and a bloom boost) is driven through the shared mood after the lighting director; the DJ's own strobe still has the last word, and a VIP show overrides it. "Reduce flashing lights" softens the strobes. The lite performance tier fires half the centre and wing emitters, shorter. `dispose()` frees the mesh, geometry and material. |

| Rave emotes | **Muzzing** (a 16-beat routine studied from a muzzing clip: hands popping to the head and jaw, fast chops across the chest, an arm flung up, stacked forearms, in a low knees-bent stance pumping on every kick with a step on the off-beat), **Hakken** (gabber kick / heel-tap footwork), **Groove** (rolling tech-house bounce) and **TikTok** (a humanoid mocap clip, `public/animations/dance_tiktok.glb`, retargeted onto the rig on load) on their own quick-bar, Shift+1–4 or Numpad 1–4; picking the active one again stops it. | Cinematic look | One full-screen pass on the shown frame, after the bloom and tone mapping, with no extra render of the scene (`src/world/cinematic.js`). It has a filmic S-curve, a split tone (cool deep-blue shadows, warm highlights) and a touch more colour. **Anamorphic lens streaks** come from the bloom's own bright pass smeared about ±100 px sideways and tinted cold, so every bright light (beam heads, the booth, the moon, strobes) throws a horizontal streak. There's also a soft round vignette, colour fringing of a few pixels toward the edges (a hair more on the kick), and fine animated grain. 2.39:1 **letterbox bars** glide in for the big moments: the arrival shot, riding the drone, a VIP show. | Drone cam | A roaming camera drone (`src/world/dronecam.js`, `DroneCamSystem`), CAM 4 of the broadcast rig, flies one of four Catmull-Rom paths — **stadium** (the default: a high aerial circling outside the bowl with the whole venue in frame — stands, pitch, roofs, both pylons, the skyline; the fog thins for far-out cameras so it stays clear), **orbit** (inside the bowl), **flythrough** (through the halo rings, low swoops over the crowd, past the VIP decks, round behind the stage) and **crane shot** — easing its aim between the DJ booth, the densest knot of dancers and a wide overview, with a little hand-held shake. | Broadcast cameras | Five cameras round the stadium and a two-output vision mixer put the show live on the venue's screens (`src/world/broadcast.js`, `BroadcastRig`). **CAM 1 · STAGE** is on the pitch in front of the barrier, centred on the booth, the lighting rig and the LED wall, on a slow dolly. **CAM 2 · CROWD** is high in the north stand, a wide down across the crowd to the stage, drifting along the stand. **CAM 3 · BOOTH** is just behind the DJ, over their shoulder, out across the decks to the crowd. **CAM 4 · DRONE** is the drone. **CAM 5 · PIT** is at eye level in the front rows of the east stand, among the dancing crowd, handheld and bouncing on the kick (the pitch is kept clear, so the crowd's floor is the stands). Two outputs each have their own WebGLRenderTarget (16:9, 640 × 360; 480 × 270 on Low, half on the lite tier: the LED wall shows it through 192 × 84 LED pixels and the picture-in-picture is at most about 600 device pixels wide). The **DJ screen** output goes to the main LED wall and the DJ booth monitor. The **raver screens** output goes to a picture on each side tower's LED screen and to everyone's picture-in-picture. Both are emissive screens with scanlines, chromatic aberration, grain, LED pixels, a "● LIVE · CAM 2 · CROWD" / timecode / GPS overlay ("E-RAVE · MORODOK TECHO") and glitch bursts on big drops. **Routing** is in the DJ panel's BROADCAST section: per output, a camera (1–5) or **AUTO**, plus the auto director's cut length (8, 16 or 32 beats). The keys are `N` (the next camera on the DJ screen), `Shift+N` (on the raver screens) and `M` (both back to AUTO). A red tally marks the camera on air. In code, `world.broadcast.setScreenSource('djScreen', 'drone')` puts a camera on a screen on that one client. The routing is server state (`shared/dronecam.js`), so every client's screens agree. The **auto director** cuts every 8/16/32 beats of the DJ's tempo on the shared clock (about 7.5 s at 128 BPM and 16 beats), so the cuts land together everywhere. Each output has its own running order and the two never show the same camera at once. Each cut flashes a short glitch. A VIP show takes both outputs to the drone's VIP shot. **Cost:** a feed render costs nearly a whole scene render (about 130 draws, 60k triangles and every stand sprite), so the outputs take turns on an even cadence and render less, not more, when the device struggles. The picture-in-picture's output leads at every 2nd frame (30 fps at 60 Hz, about 20–25 when the frame rate sags) and every 3rd on the lite tier. The LED screens' output fills the frames in between at 60 Hz, and drops to every 6th frame below 50 fps (back at 54) and every 9th on the lite tier. (Rendering the picture-in-picture every frame below 50 fps more than doubled the frame, and the performance governor answered by dropping the quality to its lite tier.) An output nobody can see doesn't render (its screens out of view and the picture-in-picture hidden). When both show the same camera, it renders once and both screens show that picture. Render layers keep the cameras from seeing the screens themselves (a feedback loop) and, except the pit cam in the crowd, give them the 3D fans' sprite stand-ins. Name tags and ravers more than 140 m away (about 2 px) sit the renders out. The crowd's LOD follows the camera on the busiest screen. The overlays redraw 5 times a second, and the DJ monitor's only for the DJ. `dispose()` frees the targets, screens and overlays. **Picture-in-picture** (`I`, the picture-in-picture button, or the panel's ×; on by default, remembered): the raver screens' feed in a neon-framed panel top left, right under the LIVE pill and above the chat. It shows "● LIVE · CAM n · NAME", the shot (the flight mode, where the camera stands, or "VIP · name" during a bottle show), the height, the drone's speed, GPS and the time, with scanlines, a slight colour split, vignette, grain and a short glitch now and then. Click or tap it to ride the drone; it steps aside while you do. The picture is drawn straight into the main canvas under the HTML frame (`world/dronepip.js`, `ui/dronepip.js`): one quad, no pixels read back from the GPU. It costs no second scene render while a side tower is in view. Otherwise the feed renders just for the panel. The panel sizes itself between the LIVE pill and the chat at its tallest: up to 340 px on a desktop, 44% of the width on a phone in portrait. On a phone held sideways there's no room, so it stays hidden and says so if you turn it on. |

## Audio

- **Default music:** **La Vida (Radio Edit)** by dbasser, Peter Guzman and Jose Alves ft. Kaala Shaw (YouTube, 123 BPM, 4:44) is the only track in the starting playlist, so it loops until the DJ queues something else; the built-in house set can be added from the DJ panel. It's `DEFAULT_TRACKS` in `shared/dj.js`, which must stay identical in the server repo. Ravers hear it through an invisible YouTube player that's already playing, quietly, in the avatar studio. If YouTube reports the video finished or can't be played, the playlist moves on (back to itself, if it's the only track).
- **YouTube playback stays smooth.** Ravers' player is a real 2 × 2 px iframe, all but transparent, behind everything, but still rendered: browsers throttle iframes that are `display: none`, zero-sized or off-screen. Every player keeps itself on the shared position. Once it has played steadily for 2 s, it seeks only if it's more than 2 s off, at most every 10 s, and backs off up to a minute when a seek doesn't take. It never seeks while buffering, because each seek stalls the player. The player reads the video's real length and sends it to the server (`track:duration`), which then advances that track on time even if nobody is listening. If the shared clock is already past the end of the video, the player reports the track as ended instead of seeking. Seeking past the end used to make YouTube restart the video and stall every 10 s. Switching tracks tears the old player down completely (blanked, removed, its timers and callbacks dropped) before the next one loads.
- **Built-in tracks** (`src/audio/synth.js`) are three procedural house tracks — piano house (organ bass, piano stabs and riff), deep house (electric-piano 9th chords, sub bass, congas) and filter disco house (sweeping chord stabs, octave bass) — with sidechain pump and vocal chops, rendered deterministically from a seed. Every client plays the same arrangement in sync with the server clock. The playlist lives in `shared/dj.js`, which must stay identical in this repo and the server repo.
- **Live analysis** (`src/audio/beat.js`, `src/audio/onset-worklet.js`):
  - Peak/RMS levels and kick detection come from an `AnalyserNode`.
  - Tempo is detected live by autocorrelating an onset envelope. The envelope is computed on the audio thread by an AudioWorklet, so it's accurate even at low frame rates; it falls back to per-frame spectral flux without HTTPS.
  - The detected tempo replaces fixed BPM values and drives a phase-locked beat clock for the dances.
- **YouTube/SoundCloud** iframes and streams without CORS can't be analysed, so the lights follow the DJ-set BPM instead.
- **The analyser is read 30 times a second**, not on every rendered frame (`ANALYSIS_HZ` in `beat.js`), with a 512-point FFT. That gives about 94 Hz bins, so the kick band (35–120 Hz) still has a bin of its own; at 128 points the bins are 375 Hz wide and the kick detector would read the snare and vocals. The beat clock still advances every frame. The LED wall's spectrum texture and the DJ's meter update only when the spectrum changes. The synth schedules notes 0.35 s ahead, so a long frame can't leave a gap in the music.

## Performance on weak hardware

`src/world/perf.js` measures real frame time and keeps phones and integrated GPUs smooth:

- **Pixel ratio cap.** High quality renders at up to a 1.75 device-pixel ratio (Medium 1.25, Low 1): sharp on high-DPI screens without paying for their full 2–3×. Dynamic resolution scales down from there when the frame rate needs it.
- **Bloom at half size.** The bloom works from half the screen's CSS size, whatever the pixel ratio, or smaller when dynamic resolution has scaled the frame down. A glow needs no high-DPI detail, and its blur passes are the heaviest post-processing.
- **Instanced and merged.** The 32 moving heads are 5 instanced draws (housings, lenses and one per beam length) instead of 96 meshes with a material each. The 12 speaker cones are one instanced draw, the speaker rings one per colour, and the stage's static boxes and truss one mesh per material. The stands, the bowl, the skyline, the lasers and confetti were already instanced or merged.
- **Dynamic resolution.** The render scale drops while FPS is under 50 and climbs back when there's headroom.
- **Stand crowd.** While FPS is under 45, the fans in the seats thin out in steps down to 25% (the 3D ones first; sprites keep at least half) and come back when it's smooth. It only changes a draw count.
- **Stand culling and LOD.** The stands are drawn in 16 slices round the bowl, each with its own bounding sphere, so frustum culling skips the ones off-screen. A lower-tier slice whose nearest edge is more than 75 m from the camera swaps its 3D fans for their sprite stand-ins (back inside 65 m). That covers the far side of the bowl, a zoomed-out camera and the drone. The 3D bubble round you in the upper tiers goes the same way. The switch happens only when a slice crosses the line, never per fan or per frame. Zoomed out or on the drone, the scene is about 60k triangles instead of about 300k.
- **A light DJ panel.** The DJ panel is an opaque dark slab, not a live blur: a 420 px blur over the animating 3D view had to be recomputed every frame, which made the DJ booth laggy. Its readouts (clock, BPM, mic meter) update 10 times a second and only write the page when the text changed, and the DJ's YouTube player has no blur behind it.
- **Festival lighting.** All unlit, additive and instanced; no real-time light count. Six curated palettes (Cyber Lotus, Emerald Acid, Liquid Gold & Midnight Chrome, Laser Cyan, Neon Sunrise, Red Room). The moving heads have drifting haze and a hot core in their beams, and new patterns: matrix (criss-cross lattice), ballyhoo (one rippling sweep) and a pinpoint that snaps every head onto one point over the floor on the drop. Drops run a strobe programme that changes every 4 bars: a chase to and fro along the rig, a wave spreading out from the booth to the pylons, split (even/odd heads trading hits), and a stutter in the last beat of each bar that chatters the blinders. Hits flip to the complementary colour (split-complementary for split), never plain white, and are off with 'Reduce flashing lights'. A dense laser fan radiates from behind the booth, and all laser sweeps are locked to the beat clock. `world/atmosphere.js` adds two layers of ground haze that catch the beams where they hit the floor, and a glow with rays behind the DJ so the booth reads as a silhouette. The new code costs about 0.4 ms of CPU per frame.
- **No per-frame scans.** Seat lookups ("E to sit", the 3D bubble, a remote player taking a seat) search a 4 m grid of the spots, not all 19,000. Picking a seat with a click casts the ray against the bowl's cross-section (`venue.js bowlRayHit`), not its mesh. Hover picks run once the mouse comes to rest, not on every move, and test one bounding sphere per raver before any mesh. Instance buffers are uploaded only when a fan actually changes (someone sits or leaves, the bubble moves), and only that slice's range.
- **No pumping.** A cut (resolution or crowd) needs a second of slow frames and comes back only after 8 s of smooth ones. A frame rate that swings settles at a lower level instead of see-sawing, which would read as flicker. The governor runs at the start of a frame, so a resolution change, which resizes and clears the canvas, never blanks a frame that's already drawn.
- **Rendering safety.** The near plane is never tiny: 0.3 m in POV, 0.5–4 m in third person (it grows with the zoom) and 1 m on the drone. The far plane is 5 km, which keeps the depth buffer precise to millimetres across the stadium. A logarithmic depth buffer was left off on purpose: it would need edits to all 15 custom shaders and costs early-depth rejection. Decals layered a centimetre or two off a surface (the feed screen on the LED wall, the booth facade and logo) use polygon offset. A sanitise pass before the bloom drops any NaN/Inf pixel and clamps HDR values, because one bad pixel would otherwise be blurred across the whole screen and blank it. The beam, halo and laser-sheet shaders are guarded against the `pow()` / `smoothstep()` edge cases that produce them.
- **Effect fallback.** Sustained FPS under 30 first turns off bloom/post-processing, then cuts heavy particles to 35% and shortens LOD ranges.
- **Recovery.** Effects come back after long smooth stretches; each recovery doubles the wait so it doesn't flap.
- **Avatar LOD.** Near avatars get the full rig. Mid-range avatars animate at a third of the rate. Far avatars become single-mesh stand-ins, and off-screen ones skip animation entirely.

The HUD stats line shows the FPS, the current resolution and the current effect tier. Settings has an "Auto performance" toggle.

## Mic and volume

- **Volume.** Music and the DJ's live mic both pass through one MasterGain node, so volume and mute control everything. YouTube/SoundCloud players follow the same volume through their own player APIs.
- **Live mic.** The DJ panel shows the microphone permission state and offers "Test mic" (works offline) and an input-device picker. It shows plain-language errors for blocked, missing or busy microphones and has a dB meter. If Web Audio can't run, the meter falls back to WebRTC stats.

## Fonts

Khmer text uses Kantumruy Pro and Koulen from Google Fonts. The Khmer subsets are preloaded before any canvas draws them.

Windows has no flag-emoji glyphs, so `country-flag-emoji-polyfill` loads a flags-only font there. It's self-hosted as `public/TwemojiCountryFlags.woff2`; Twemoji graphics are by Twitter and licensed CC-BY 4.0.
